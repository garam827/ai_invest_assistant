Analysts see continued risk premium with Russia’s export reductions (rumored 13–20% in July) and near-shipping halts. - Price targets on wheat: Some projections point toward KC hard red wheat near $7.50, with a key support around $6.39 (July 9 low) to maintain upside potential. - Corn dynamics: Corn attempting to follow wheat but faces resistance near key moving averages; stocks-to-use ratio has tightened (about 20%), raising questions about rebuilding stocks after multiple high-yield years. - Soybeans: Soybeans show spillover strength from wheat and ongoing demand, but $12 resistance remains tough; potential for a rally through $12 if heat pressure widens in early August. - Inflation and dollar influence: Cooling inflation (CPI 3.5%, PPI 5.5%, core 4.7%) may weaken the dollar in the near term, supporting grains. Energy price declines contributed to lower input costs, though geopolitical tensions keep some volatility in energy and fertilizer markets. - Cattle note: Cattle futures weakening, with expectations of new lows for October live cattle and potential support near $218–$220. Bottom line: The market is pricing in risk from supply disruptions (notably wheat due to Black Sea tensions), with wheat leading the rally and corn/soybeans showing mixed follow-through. Near-term dynamics will depend on export flows, inflation/dollar trends, and input costs.
